# Dataset Documentation

# Correlation Mapping of Sanitation Access and Public Health Outcomes in Tamil Nadu

## Dataset Overview

This dataset was compiled to analyze the relationship between sanitation access and public health outcomes across districts of Tamil Nadu. The dataset integrates sanitation indicators, health indicators, socio-demographic variables, and spatial information from multiple government and public sources.

## Study Area

- State: Tamil Nadu, India
- Unit of Analysis: District Level
- Geographic Scope: All districts of Tamil Nadu

---

# Dataset Categories

## 1. Sanitation Indicators

The sanitation dataset includes:

- Household toilet coverage (%)
- Open defecation rate
- Availability of sanitation facilities
- Community sanitation infrastructure
- Open Defecation Free (ODF) status

### Sources

- Swachh Bharat Mission (SBM) Dashboard
- National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5)
- Tamil Nadu Open Government Data (OGD) Portal

---

## 2. Public Health Indicators

Health-related variables include:

- Infant Mortality Rate (IMR)
- Under-5 Mortality Rate
- Acute Diarrheal Disease (ADD) incidence
- Cholera incidence
- Waterborne disease statistics
- District public health records

### Sources

- District Health Reports
- Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme (IDSP)
- Tamil Nadu Health Department

---

## 3. Socio-Demographic Indicators

Socio-economic variables considered:

- Literacy rate
- Per-capita income
- Urban population ratio
- Rural population ratio
- Population characteristics

### Sources

- Census of India
- National Sample Survey (NSS)
- District Statistical Handbooks
- NFHS Data

---

## 4. Spatial Dataset

Geospatial information used for GIS mapping:

- District boundary shapefiles
- Administrative boundaries
- Spatial reference layers

### Sources

- Tamil Nadu State GIS Database
- Bhuvan GIS Portal (ISRO)

---

# Data Collection Process

1. Collection of sanitation statistics from official government sources.
2. Collection of district-level health outcome data.
3. Acquisition of socio-demographic indicators.
4. Integration of GIS boundary datasets.
5. Data cleaning and preprocessing.
6. Standardization and validation of variables.

---

# Data Preprocessing

The following preprocessing steps were performed:

- Missing value handling
- Duplicate record removal
- Data consistency checks
- Variable standardization
- District name normalization
- Dataset integration from multiple sources

# Intended Use

This dataset supports:

- Correlation analysis
- Regression modeling
- GIS-based spatial analysis
- Public health studies
- Sanitation policy research
- Health inequality assessment

---

# Data Limitations

- District-level aggregation may mask local variations.
- Some variables originate from self-reported surveys.
- Temporal differences may exist between data sources.
- Public datasets may contain reporting inconsistencies.
- Correlation does not imply causation.
