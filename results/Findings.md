# 📊 Findings and Results

## Overview

This document summarizes the key findings obtained from the statistical analysis, GIS-based spatial assessment, and correlation mapping conducted as part of the research project:

> **Correlation Mapping of Sanitation Access and Public Health Outcomes in Tamil Nadu**

The analysis examined the relationship between sanitation infrastructure, public health indicators, and socio-demographic factors across districts of Tamil Nadu.

The findings provide evidence-based insights into how sanitation access influences health outcomes and identifies regions requiring targeted interventions.

---

# 🎯 Research Questions Addressed

The study aimed to answer the following key questions:

* Does sanitation access influence public health outcomes?
* Is there a relationship between sanitation coverage and infant mortality?
* How do socio-demographic factors affect sanitation adoption?
* Can GIS mapping reveal regional sanitation disparities?
* What policy recommendations emerge from the analysis?

---

# 🚻 Sanitation Coverage Assessment

## Major Observation

The analysis indicates that Tamil Nadu has made substantial progress in sanitation infrastructure development, particularly through initiatives such as the Swachh Bharat Mission (SBM).

### Key Findings

* Household toilet coverage has significantly increased across districts.
* Most districts report high sanitation infrastructure availability.
* Open Defecation Free (ODF) status has been achieved in several regions.
* Sanitation accessibility has improved considerably compared to previous years.

### Interpretation

The results suggest that government sanitation programs have successfully expanded sanitation infrastructure throughout the state.

However, infrastructure availability alone does not guarantee consistent usage.

---

# 📉 Relationship Between Sanitation and Infant Mortality

One of the primary objectives of the study was to evaluate the relationship between sanitation access and infant mortality rates.

## Findings

Districts with higher toilet coverage generally exhibit:

* Lower Infant Mortality Rates (IMR)
* Better child health outcomes
* Reduced exposure to environmental health risks

Conversely, districts with lower sanitation access tend to report:

* Higher infant mortality
* Increased disease burden
* Greater public health vulnerability

### Interpretation

The findings indicate that improved sanitation infrastructure contributes positively to child health and survival outcomes.

Better sanitation reduces exposure to pathogens responsible for various preventable diseases that disproportionately affect infants and children.

---

# 🦠 Waterborne Disease Analysis

The study also examined the relationship between sanitation indicators and waterborne disease prevalence.

## Findings

Districts experiencing lower sanitation accessibility show:

* Increased diarrheal disease incidence
* Greater prevalence of waterborne illnesses
* Higher public health risk levels

Areas with stronger sanitation infrastructure generally report:

* Lower disease incidence
* Improved hygiene conditions
* Better environmental health outcomes

### Interpretation

The analysis confirms that sanitation remains a critical determinant of disease prevention and public health improvement.

---

# 🔗 Correlation Analysis Results

Pearson Correlation Analysis was performed to measure relationships between sanitation indicators and health outcomes.

## Key Observations

### Strong Negative Correlation

A strong negative relationship was observed between:

* Toilet Coverage
* Infant Mortality Rate

This indicates that increases in sanitation coverage are generally associated with reductions in infant mortality.

---

### Negative Correlation

Negative correlations were also identified between:

* Sanitation Coverage
* Waterborne Disease Incidence

Districts with better sanitation facilities generally experience lower disease prevalence.

---

### Positive Correlation

Positive relationships were observed between:

* Open Defecation Rates
* Disease Incidence

Higher levels of open defecation correspond to increased public health risks.

---

# 📊 Regression Analysis Results

Multiple Linear Regression was conducted to evaluate the influence of sanitation and socio-demographic factors on public health outcomes.

## Independent Variables

* Sanitation Coverage
* Literacy Rate
* Per Capita Income

## Dependent Variable

* Infant Mortality Rate (IMR)

---

## Findings

The regression results indicate that:

### 🚻 Sanitation Coverage

Sanitation coverage emerged as one of the strongest predictors of improved public health outcomes.

Higher sanitation coverage was associated with:

* Lower IMR
* Reduced disease incidence
* Better overall health indicators

---

### 📚 Literacy Rate

Higher literacy rates were associated with:

* Better sanitation adoption
* Increased hygiene awareness
* Improved health outcomes

---

### 💰 Income Level

Income was found to influence:

* Access to sanitation facilities
* Healthcare accessibility
* Living conditions

Districts with higher income levels generally reported better sanitation usage and health outcomes.

---

# 👥 Socio-Demographic Insights

The analysis highlights the importance of socio-economic factors in shaping sanitation behavior.

## Major Findings

Districts with:

* Higher literacy levels
* Better economic conditions
* Greater urbanization

tend to achieve better sanitation and health outcomes.

---

## Interpretation

Sanitation effectiveness is influenced not only by infrastructure availability but also by education, awareness, and economic capacity.

This suggests that sanitation interventions should be combined with broader social development programs.

---

# 🚻 Open Defecation and Behavioral Gaps

Despite improvements in toilet coverage, the study identified continued behavioral challenges.

## Findings

Certain districts continue to exhibit:

* Open defecation practices
* Inconsistent sanitation usage
* Behavioral resistance to facility adoption

---

## Contributing Factors

Potential reasons include:

* Cultural practices
* Water availability issues
* Poor maintenance of facilities
* Limited sanitation awareness

---

## Interpretation

The results suggest that infrastructure development alone is insufficient.

Behavioral change programs and community awareness initiatives are equally important for achieving sustainable sanitation outcomes.
